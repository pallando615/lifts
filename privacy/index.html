Lifts Privacy Policy

Last updated 11 November 2026

The short version. Lifts keeps your workouts on your iPhone. I, the developer, don't collect, receive or share any information about you. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and Lifts contains no third-party code.

What Lifts stores

Your exercises, templates, workouts, sets, notes and settings live in Lifts's private storage on your iPhone. Nothing leaves your device unless you use one of the features described below.

Apple Health

Lifts asks permission to read three kinds of data from Apple Health: workouts, heart rate and active energy. It only ever reads. It never writes anything to Health.

  • Why. To find the Strength Training workout that matches a workout you logged in Lifts, and show its duration, active calories and heart rate next to your sets.
  • What is kept. When a match is found, Lifts saves a short summary on your iPhone, next to that workout: when the Health workout started and ended, its active calories, and its average and maximum heart rate. This summary stays on that iPhone and is never synced to iCloud. The heart rate chart is read from Health each time you open it and isn't saved.
  • What is never done. Lifts doesn't share Health data with anyone, doesn't use it for advertising, marketing or data mining, and doesn't send it to me.
  • Your choice. Connecting Health is optional, and everything else in Lifts works without it. You can change or withdraw access at any time in the Health app: tap your profile picture, then Apps, then Lifts.

iCloud

If you are signed in to iCloud, Lifts syncs your library between your devices through your private iCloud database for Lifts (CloudKit). It is on by default, and you can turn it off in Lifts under Settings, iCloud. It covers your workouts, sets, notes, exercises, templates and pinned lifts. It never includes Health data: the Health summaries described above stay on the iPhone that saved them.

That data is stored in your own iCloud account. I can't see it, and Apple's terms and privacy policy apply to it. Turning sync off stops syncing but doesn't delete what is already in iCloud. To delete it, open Settings on your iPhone, tap your name, choose iCloud, and manage the storage used by Lifts there.

Exports and backups

CSV exports and backup files are created only when you ask for them, and they go only where you send them: Files, AirDrop, Mail and so on. Lifts doesn't upload them anywhere. A backup contains your whole library, including the saved Health summaries, so keep it somewhere you trust.

Home Screen widget

The Up Next widget shows a small summary that Lifts saves in a storage area shared with the widget on your iPhone: the next template's name and exercises, when you last did it, and your accent color. It never leaves your device.

Analytics and crash reports

Lifts has none of its own. If you chose to share iPhone analytics with app developers (Settings, Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements), Apple may give me anonymous crash reports and usage counts through App Store Connect. They don't identify you.

Children

Lifts doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Keeping and deleting your data

Because I hold none of your data, there is nothing for me to delete, correct or hand over. Your data is on your devices and in your iCloud, and you control it: delete a workout in History, or delete Lifts to remove its data from your iPhone, and remove what is in iCloud as described above.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too, and the new version is the one that applies.
